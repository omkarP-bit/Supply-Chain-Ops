# Final Submission Document Guidelines

These guidelines are for all teams submitting their final short document. Each team must explain its selected problem statement, understanding of the problem, proposed idea, solution approach, and MVP clearly and concisely.

The expected submission length is **2 pages**. The document must be **strictly under 3 pages maximum**. Submissions exceeding the page limit may not be considered for evaluation.

## Submission Objective

The final document should answer the following clearly:

> What problem did the team understand, what solution is the team proposing, and what MVP will the team build?

The document should not simply repeat the problem statement. It should show how well the team has understood the PS and how practically they plan to solve it.

## Mandatory Document Requirements

- Preferred length: **2 pages**
- Maximum length: **strictly under 3 pages**
- Format: **Markdown file (`.md`)**
- Language: Simple, clear, and professional
- Content: Concise and relevant to the selected problem statement
- Diagrams: Allowed only if they help explain the idea or solution
- Code snippets: Avoid long code snippets
- Appendices: Not allowed beyond the page limit

## Required Sections

### 1. Team Details

Include the following information:

- Team name

### 2. Problem Statement in Short

Briefly mention the selected problem statement in your own words.

This section should be short and should explain:

- What the PS is about
- The main challenge described in the PS

Do not copy the full problem statement directly. Summarize it clearly.

### 3. Understanding of the Problem Statement

Explain how your team understood the problem.

This section should show:

- What exact problem you are solving
- Which specific part of the PS your team is focusing on

This is an important section because it helps evaluate how deeply the team has understood the PS, not just the solution idea.

### 4. Idea Summary

Explain your core idea in simple terms.

Include:

- What your idea is
- What your solution will do
- How it connects to the selected PS
- What makes the idea useful, practical, or unique

Keep this section direct and easy to understand.

### 5. Proposed Solution

Describe how the solution will work.

You may include:

- Main features of the solution
- Basic user flow
- System workflow or process flow
- Technologies, tools, or platforms planned
- Any assumptions made by the team
- How the solution addresses the problem identified in the PS

Focus on clarity and feasibility. Avoid unnecessary theory.

### 6. MVP Description

Clearly define the Minimum Viable Product that the team will build.

Mention:

- Core features included in the MVP
- What users will be able to do with the MVP
- What will be demonstrated during the final presentation
- What will not be included in the first version
- How the MVP proves the proposed solution

The MVP should be realistic and achievable within the available time.

### 7. Impact and Feasibility

Briefly explain:

- Expected impact of the solution
- Why the solution is practical
- How feasible it is to build the MVP
- Possible challenges, risks, or limitations
- How the team may handle those challenges

## Suggested Page Structure

### Page 1

- Team details
- Problem statement in short
- Understanding of the problem statement
- Idea summary

### Page 2

- Proposed solution
- MVP description
- Impact and feasibility

### Optional Extra Space Before Page 3

Use only if absolutely required for:

- A simple architecture diagram
- A process flow
- A visual explanation of the MVP

The complete document must still remain strictly under 3 pages.

## Evaluation Focus

The submission may be evaluated based on:

- Clarity of problem statement understanding
- Relevance of the idea to the selected PS
- Practicality of the proposed solution
- Clarity and feasibility of the MVP
- Innovation and usefulness of the idea
- Expected impact
- Quality, structure, and conciseness of the document

## Important Instructions for Teams

- Be specific and avoid vague explanations.
- Do not fill pages with generic content.
- Do not copy large portions of the PS directly.
- Keep the document focused on your selected problem and your proposed solution.
- Clearly separate idea, solution, and MVP.
- Use headings and bullet points for readability.
- Ensure the document can be understood by someone reading it for the first time.

## Final Checklist Before Submission

Before submitting, each team should confirm:

- [ ] The document is preferably 2 pages.
- [ ] The document is strictly under 3 pages.
- [ ] The selected PS is mentioned in short.
- [ ] The team has explained its understanding of the PS in its own words.
- [ ] The idea is explained clearly.
- [ ] The proposed solution is described properly.
- [ ] The MVP is clearly defined.
- [ ] The document explains what will be demonstrated in the MVP.
- [ ] The impact and feasibility are briefly covered.
- [ ] The document avoids unnecessary theory and long code snippets.
- [ ] The final file is submitted as a Markdown file (`.md`).
